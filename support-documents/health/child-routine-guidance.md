Medical guidelines and international standards prioritize neuromuscular development and metabolic health over anaerobic performance for children.

### World Health Organization (WHO) Standards
The WHO establishes guidelines based on age groups, focusing on the accumulation of physical activity:

* **Ages 5 to 17:** A minimum of **60 minutes per day** of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, mostly aerobic.
* **Strengthening Frequency:** At least **3 days per week** should include activities that strengthen the musculoskeletal system.
* **Focus:** At this age, "strengthening" does not imply heavy weightlifting; it refers to bodyweight exercises, jumping, and resistance-based play.

### American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) Recommendations
The AAP supports resistance training in children provided specific safety protocols are met:

* **Emotional Maturity:** The child must be able to follow precise instructions. This usually occurs around **7 or 8 years old**.
* **Technique over Load:** Maximum repetition ($1RM$) testing is discouraged. The focus remains on motor coordination and movement patterns.
* **Supervision:** A high instructor-to-student ratio is required to correct posture and form in real-time.

### Standard Routine Components
According to the *American College of Sports Medicine* (ACSM) and the AAP, a play-based routine should be structured as follows:

| Phase | Objective | Activity Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Warm-up** | Dynamic preparation | Tag games, active joint mobility. |
| **Motor Skills** | Coordination and balance | Obstacle courses, lateral hops, single-leg balance. |
| **Resistance** | Functional strength | Squats ("frog jumps"), knee push-ups, climbing. |
| **Cool-down** | Recovery | Gentle stretching and breathing control. |

### Key Considerations
1.  **Avoid Early Specialization:** Variety in movement prevents overuse injuries and improves overall physical literacy.
2.  **Growth:** Supervised strength training does not damage growth plates; rather, it increases bone mineral density.
3.  **Thermoregulation:** Children have a lower sweating capacity than adults. Routines must ensure frequent breaks and constant hydration.
